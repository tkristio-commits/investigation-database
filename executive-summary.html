Pelajaran dari 183 Investigasi

Apa yang dikatakan faktor kontribusi kecelakaan dan serious incident penerbangan sipil Indonesia — hasil investigasi KNKT 2007–2024 — tentang di mana risiko terkonsentrasi, dan apa yang berada dalam kendali kru di kokpit.

Setiap laporan akhir KNKT menyebut satu atau lebih contributing factor. Ke-376 faktor dari 183 investigasi telah dibaca satu per satu dan dipetakan ke struktur tiga tingkat domain → disiplin → elemen milik taksonomi CAST/ICAO. Rekap di bawah ini menghitung seberapa sering sebuah jenis faktor muncul — bukan bobot penyebab — sehingga yang terlihat adalah pola yang berulang lintas maskapai, jenis pesawat, dan dekade.

Satu pesan menonjol: sebagian besar rantai kejadian melewati titik-titik keputusan dan penerbangan yang lazim, bukan kegagalan luar biasa. Itu kabar baik — artinya banyak di antaranya dapat diputus oleh kru yang terlatih dan disiplin pada prosedur.

01

Di mana faktor terkonsentrasi

Faktor manusia / individu218 · 58%
Faktor peralatan66 · 18%
Faktor organisasi47 · 13%
Lingkungan operasi37 · 10%
Tidak dapat ditentukan8 · 2%

Faktor manusia mendominasi, tetapi jarang berdiri sendiri: faktor peralatan (kegagalan powerplant dan airframe) sering menjadi pemicu, sedangkan faktor organisasi — prosedur, pelatihan, pengawasan — menentukan seberapa siap kru menghadapinya. Cuaca dan infrastruktur bandara melengkapi konteksnya.

02

Faktor manusia: pola yang berulang

Dari 218 faktor manusia, 123 berada di disiplin Procedural / Task Performance — pelaksanaan tugas dan kepatuhan SOP. Rinciannya:

Tindakan / kelalaian (handling, SOP)75
Pemrosesan informasi & pengambilan keputusan38
Koordinasi / CRM18
Kesadaran situasional (perceptual)14
Perencanaan / persiapan10
Monitoring (pilot flying / pilot monitoring)7
Disorientasi spasial / ilusi7

Kelompok terbesar bukan pengetahuan yang kurang, melainkan eksekusi — menyimpang dari teknik atau SOP yang sudah diketahui — diikuti oleh keputusan untuk melanjutkan saat pilihan yang lebih aman tersedia (mis. melanjutkan approach yang tidak stabil alih-alih go-around). Koordinasi kru dan pembagian tugas yang tidak efektif adalah tema ketiga.

03

Jenis kejadian yang paling sering

Dihitung per investigasi (bukan per faktor):

Runway Excursion (RE)53
Controlled Flight into Terrain (CFIT)29
Kegagalan sistem — powerplant (SCF-PP)19
Kegagalan sistem — non-powerplant (SCF-NP)16

Operasi landas pacu adalah medan risiko terbesar. Runway excursion, abnormal runway contact, runway incursion, dan ground collision bersama-sama mencakup 76 dari 183 kejadian (≈42%). Sebagian besar berakar pada fase approach dan landing: kecepatan/energi berlebih, approach tidak stabil, teknik flare, dan keputusan go-around yang tertunda.

CFIT dan LOC-I — jarang, tapi konsekuensinya tertinggi

33 kejadian gabungan. Secara global keduanya adalah kategori dengan fatalitas tertinggi (ICAO / IATA). Dalam data ini CFIT sangat lekat dengan operasi ke bandara terpencil dan berterain di Papua, Sulawesi, dan Kalimantan — kondisi cuaca menurun, dokumentasi approach terbatas, dan hilangnya kesadaran terrain.

04

Apa artinya bagi Anda di kokpit

Tujuh perilaku yang, menurut pola di atas, paling sering menjadi pembeda:

Prioritas berbasis frekuensi faktor

1   Disiplin stabilised approach & go-around

Tema tunggal yang paling berulang. Tetapkan gate dan patuhi — go-around dari approach tidak stabil atau bounce yang berat bukan kegagalan, itu prosedur.

2   Manajemen energi saat approach & landing

Penyumbang utama runway excursion: kecepatan tinggi, touchdown jauh, pengereman terlambat, dan aquaplaning di landas pacu basah. Hitung ulang landing distance bila kondisi berubah.

3   Terrain awareness pada operasi non-precision & bandara terpencil

Patuhi MSA/MDA, jangan turun tanpa referensi visual yang sah, hormati EGPWS/TAWS tanpa negosiasi. Waspadai transisi VMC ke IMC.

4   Keputusan “melanjutkan vs berhenti”

38 faktor pengambilan keputusan. Sebagian besar adalah keputusan untuk terus — diverging, hold, atau divert lebih dini sering menjadi opsi yang terlewat.

5   CRM: pembagian tugas & assertiveness

18 faktor koordinasi. Briefing yang eksplisit, callout yang jelas, dan kesediaan PM untuk menantang PF adalah pertahanan yang murah dan efektif.

6   Monitoring aktif & pencegahan penyimpangan otomasi

Peran PM bukan pasif. Pantau flight path, mode, dan energi; sebut penyimpangan lebih awal sebelum menjadi upset.

7   Kelelahan & kesiapan fisik

Muncul lebih jarang (faktor fatigue & kondisi fisik) tetapi nyata, termasuk kasus terkini. Gunakan rest period secara benar dan laporkan bila tidak fit.

05

Catatan data & keterbatasan

  • Angka menghitung frekuensi kemunculan jenis faktor, bukan bobot kausal. Satu kejadian berkontribusi beberapa faktor; kejadian dengan laporan panjang otomatis menyumbang lebih banyak baris.
  • Taksonomi CAST/ICAO Air Traffic Causal & Contributory Factors aslinya ditulis untuk kejadian ATC; cabang manusia, organisasi, peralatan, dan lingkungan diterapkan pada tindakan kru dan perawatan secara analogi.
  • 8 faktor berstatus tidak dapat ditentukan — laporan ringkas atau “NIL” tanpa teks yang bisa dianalisis.
  • Cakupan terbatas pada investigasi yang telah Final dan terpublikasi di laman KNKT. Tahun-tahun terbaru masih akan bertambah seiring laporan diterbitkan.
  • Kejadian dengan dua alur kausal yang sama kuat (mis. keputusan cuaca plus kegagalan sistem) dikode ke alur yang dominan.

Executive Summary  ·  for members of the Indonesian Pilots' Association (IPI)

Lessons from 183 Investigations

What the contributing factors of Indonesian civil-aviation accidents and serious incidents — from KNKT investigations, 2007–2024 — say about where the risk concentrates, and how much of it sits within the crew's control on the flight deck.

Source KNKT investigations (Final Reports) Period 2007–2024 Coverage 183 occurrences · 376 factors Operators 85 · 61 aircraft types Taxonomy CAST/ICAO AT C&CF v1.0.1 Compiled August 2026
58%of factors trace to people
76occurrences involving runway operations

75“action / inaction” factors

Every KNKT final report names one or more contributing factors. All 376 factors from 183 investigations have been read individually and mapped onto the three-tier domain → discipline → element structure of the CAST/ICAO taxonomy. The tallies below count how often a type of factor appears — not its causal weight — so what stands out are the patterns that recur across operators, aircraft types, and decades.

One message stands out: most occurrence chains ran through ordinary decision points and ordinary flying, not exotic failures. That is good news — it means many of them can be broken by a crew that is well trained and disciplined about procedure.

01

Where the factors concentrate

Individual / human factors218 · 58%
Equipment factors66 · 18%
Organizational factors47 · 13%
Operating environment37 · 10%
Undetermined8 · 2%

Human factors dominate, but rarely stand alone: equipment factors (powerplant and airframe failures) are often the trigger, while organizational factors — procedures, training, oversight — determine how ready the crew is to handle it. Weather and aerodrome infrastructure fill in the context.

02

Human factors: the recurring patterns

Of the 218 human factors, 123 sit in the Procedural / Task Performance discipline — task execution and SOP compliance. The breakdown:

Action / inaction (handling, SOP)75
Information processing & decision making38
Coordination / CRM18
Situational awareness (perceptual)14
Planning / preparation10
Monitoring (pilot flying / pilot monitoring)7
Spatial disorientation / illusions7

The largest group is not missing knowledge but execution — deviating from a technique or SOP that was already known — followed by the decision to continue when a safer option was available (for example, pressing on with an unstable approach instead of going around). Ineffective crew coordination and task sharing is the third theme.

03

The most frequent occurrence types

Counted per investigation (not per factor):

Runway Excursion (RE)53
Controlled Flight into Terrain (CFIT)29
System failure — powerplant (SCF-PP)19
System failure — non-powerplant (SCF-NP)16
Abnormal Runway Contact (ARC)15
Ground Collision (GCOL)7
Loss of Control – Inflight (LOC-I)4

Runway operations are the largest field of risk. Runway excursion, abnormal runway contact, runway incursion, and ground collision together account for 76 of 183 occurrences (≈42%). Most are rooted in the approach and landing phase: excess speed / energy, unstable approaches, flare technique, and a delayed go-around decision.

CFIT and LOC-I — rare, but the highest consequence

33 occurrences combined. Globally these are the categories with the highest fatality toll (ICAO / IATA). In this data, CFIT is closely tied to operations into remote, terrain-bound aerodromes in Papua, Sulawesi, and Kalimantan — deteriorating weather, limited approach documentation, and a loss of terrain awareness.

04

What this means for you on the flight deck

Seven behaviours that, on the patterns above, most often make the difference:

Priorities ranked by factor frequency

1   Stabilised approach & go-around discipline

The single most recurring theme. Set the gate and hold to it — a go-around from an unstable approach or a heavy bounce is not a failure, it is the procedure.

2   Energy management on approach & landing

The leading driver of runway excursions: high speed, long touchdown, late braking, and aquaplaning on a wet runway. Recompute landing distance when the conditions change.

3   Terrain awareness on non-precision ops & remote aerodromes

Respect MSA/MDA, do not descend without a valid visual reference, and act on EGPWS/TAWS without negotiation. Watch the VMC-to-IMC transition.

4   The “continue vs. stop” decision

38 decision-making factors. Most were decisions to press on — diverting, holding, or an earlier divert were often the options that were missed.

5   CRM: task sharing & assertiveness

18 coordination factors. An explicit briefing, clear callouts, and a PM willing to challenge the PF are a cheap and effective defence.

6   Active monitoring & automation-deviation prevention

The PM role is not passive. Monitor flight path, mode, and energy; call the deviation early, before it becomes an upset.

7   Fatigue & physical fitness

Appears less often (fatigue and physical-condition factors) but it is real, including recent cases. Use rest periods properly and report when you are not fit to fly.

05

Data notes & limitations

  • The numbers count the frequency of appearance of a factor type, not causal weight. A single occurrence contributes several factors; occurrences with long reports automatically contribute more rows.
  • The CAST/ICAO Air Traffic Causal & Contributory Factors taxonomy was originally written for ATC events; its human, organizational, equipment, and environment branches are applied to flight-crew and maintenance actions by analogy.
  • 8 factors are marked undetermined — short-form or “NIL” reports with no analysable text.
  • Coverage is limited to investigations that are Final and published on the KNKT website. Recent years will still grow as reports are issued.
  • Occurrences with two equally strong causal threads (e.g. a weather decision plus a system failure) are coded to the dominant one.
